# Editorial Product Information: Wyost (denosumab)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis has been compiled from the following provided official Product Information (PI) documents:
    - **Australian (TGA)**: Wyost (denosumab) Solution for Injection
    - **US (FDA)**: Wyost (denosumab-bbdz) injection, for subcutaneous use
- **Purpose**: To provide healthcare professionals with a harmonised editorial overview of the drug's properties, clinical use, and regulatory differences across jurisdictions.
- **Note**: This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference. It is not a substitute for the official PI and should not be used for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction without consulting the local approved documentation.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Denosumab (a human IgG2 monoclonal antibody) [Australian]; Denosumab-bbdz [US].
- **Chemical Class**: RANK ligand (RANKL) inhibitor.
- **Brand Name**: **Wyost**.
- **Biosimilarity**: Wyost is a biosimilar to the reference medicine **Xgeva**. 
- **ATC Code**: M05BX04.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**: 
    - **Solution for Injection**: 120 mg/1.7 mL (70 mg/mL) in a single-dose vial.
- **Route of Administration**: Subcutaneous (SC) injection only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Sandoz Pty Ltd [Australian]; Sandoz Inc [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Prevention of Skeletal Related Events (SREs)**: Indicated for use in patients with multiple myeloma and in patients with bone metastases from solid tumours.
- **Giant Cell Tumour of Bone (GCTB)**: Indicated for the treatment of adults and skeletally mature adolescents with GCTB that is unresectable, or where surgical resection is likely to result in severe morbidity.
- **Hypercalcaemia of Malignancy (HCM)**: Indicated for the treatment of HCM refractory to intravenous bisphosphonate therapy.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Administration**: Must be administered by a healthcare professional. SC injection only into the upper arm, upper thigh, or abdomen. Do not administer intravenously, intramuscularly, or intradermally.
- **Prevention of SREs**: 120 mg administered once every 4 weeks.
- **Giant Cell Tumour of Bone and Hypercalcaemia of Malignancy**: 120 mg administered once every 4 weeks, with additional **loading doses** of 120 mg on Days 8 and 15 of the first month of treatment.

### Supplementation
- Patients must be adequately supplemented with calcium and vitamin D to prevent or treat hypocalcaemia, unless hypercalcaemia is present. 
- [Australian]: Daily supplementation with at least 500 mg calcium and 400 IU vitamin D is required.

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect for particulate matter and discolouration. The solution should be clear, colourless to slightly yellowish/brownish [Australian] or opalescent [US].
- Allow the vial to reach room temperature (up to 25°C) before injecting (takes approx. 15–30 minutes).
- Do not shake. Use a 27-gauge needle for administration.
- Single-use only; discard any unused portion.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypocalcaemia**: Pre-existing hypocalcaemia must be corrected prior to initiating therapy.
- **Hypersensitivity**: Known clinically significant hypersensitivity to denosumab or any of the excipients (including CHO-derived proteins).
- **Pregnancy**: [Australian-approved; not listed as a contraindication in the US Highlights, though contraindicated in US Section 8.1].
- **Unhealed Dental Lesions**: Unhealed lesions from dental or oral surgery [Australian-specific contraindication].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Drug Products with Same Active Ingredient**: Patients should not receive Wyost concomitantly with other denosumab-containing products (e.g., those used for osteoporosis).
- **Hypocalcaemia**: Can be severe and fatal. Risk is increased in patients with renal impairment (CrCl < 30 mL/min) or on dialysis. Monitor calcium levels, especially in the first weeks of therapy.
- **Osteonecrosis of the Jaw (ONJ)**: Reported frequently. Prior to treatment, perform an oral examination and appropriate preventive dentistry. Avoid invasive dental procedures during treatment.
- **Atypical Femoral Fractures**: Low-trauma subtrochanteric or diaphyseal fractures have occurred. Evaluate patients with new or unusual thigh, hip, or groin pain.
- **Hypercalcaemia Following Discontinuation**: Clinically significant hypercalcaemia, sometimes requiring hospitalisation and complicated by acute renal injury, has been reported in patients with GCTB and those with growing skeletons weeks to months after stopping treatment.
- **Multiple Vertebral Fractures (MVF) Following Discontinuation**: Risk is higher in patients with osteoporosis or prior fractures. Advise patients not to interrupt therapy without medical advice.
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity**: Can cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status before initiation. Advise females of reproductive potential to use effective contraception during and for at least 5 months after the last dose.
- **Excipients**: Contains sorbitol; patients with rare hereditary fructose intolerance should not use this medicine [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Reactions (≥10%)
- **General**: Fatigue, asthenia, peripheral oedema, pyrexia.
- **Gastrointestinal**: Nausea, diarrhoea, constipation, vomiting, abdominal pain.
- **Investigations**: Hypophosphataemia (very common in HCM, up to 76%), hypocalcaemia, weight decrease.
- **Musculoskeletal**: Back pain, arthralgia, pain in extremity, musculoskeletal pain.
- **Respiratory**: Dyspnoea, cough, upper respiratory tract infection.
- **Neurological/Psychiatric**: Headache, insomnia.
- **Haematological**: Anaemia, thrombocytopenia [US-noted in Multiple Myeloma].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Osteonecrosis of the Jaw**: Incidence increases with duration of exposure; adjusted incidence up to 4.6% per year [Australian].
- **Severe Hypocalcaemia**: Corrected serum calcium < 1.75 mmol/L (7 mg/dL) occurred in 3.1% of patients [US].
- **Atypical Femoral Fractures**: Reported uncommonly in cancer trials but up to 3.9% in long-term GCTB follow-up [Australian].
- **Anaphylaxis**: Rare but clinically significant hypersensitivity.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Drug-Drug Interactions**: No formal studies have been conducted.
- **Chemotherapy/Hormone Therapy**: Pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics were not altered by concomitant chemotherapy and/or hormone therapy.
- **Bisphosphonates**: [Australian]: Denosumab should not be administered concomitantly with bisphosphonates. [US]: Pharmacodynamic effects were similar in patients with or without prior IV bisphosphonate exposure.
- **Calcimimetics**: Concomitant use with drugs that lower calcium levels may increase the risk of hypocalcaemia; monitor serum calcium closely [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **Australian Category D**: Contraindicated for use during pregnancy.
    - **US Classification**: No lettered category; however, the PI states it "can cause fetal harm" and is based on animal data showing increased fetal loss and developmental abnormalities.
- **Breastfeeding**: It is unknown if denosumab is excreted in human milk. A decision should be made to discontinue nursing or the drug, taking into account the importance of the drug to the mother. Maternal IgG is known to be excreted in human milk.
- **Fertility**: No human data available. Animal studies in monkeys showed no effect on male or female fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Not recommended for patients < 18 years, except for skeletally mature adolescents with GCTB (aged 12–17 years). Risks include impairment of bone growth and tooth eruption.
- **Elderly Use (≥ 65 years)**: No overall differences in safety or efficacy observed; no dose adjustment required.
- **Renal Impairment**: No dose adjustment necessary. Patients with CrCl < 30 mL/min or receiving dialysis are at significantly higher risk for hypocalcaemia.
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Safety and efficacy have not been studied [Australian]. The drug is not expected to be eliminated via hepatic metabolic mechanisms [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Denosumab binds with high affinity and specificity to RANKL, preventing the RANKL/RANK interaction. This inhibits osteoclast formation, function, and survival, thereby reducing bone resorption and cancer-induced bone destruction.
- **Pharmacodynamics**: Causes rapid reduction in bone resorption markers (e.g., uNTx/Cr) by approximately 80% within 1 week of administration.
- **Pharmacokinetics**: 
    - **Bioavailability**: 62% after SC injection.
    - **Accumulation**: Approx. 2-fold accumulation with 120 mg every 4 weeks dosing; steady state reached by 6 months.
    - **Metabolism**: Degraded into small peptides and amino acids via typical immunoglobulin clearance pathways.
    - **Elimination Half-life**: Mean of 28 days.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Bone Metastases from Solid Tumours**: Three randomised, double-blind trials (Breast Cancer, Prostate Cancer, and Other Solid Tumours/Multiple Myeloma) demonstrated Wyost was non-inferior (and in some cases superior) to zoledronic acid in delaying the time to the first on-study SRE.
- **Multiple Myeloma**: An international trial in newly diagnosed patients showed denosumab was non-inferior to zoledronic acid in delaying time to first SRE.
- **Giant Cell Tumour of Bone**: Two open-label, single-arm trials showed an objective response rate of 25% (RECIST 1.1) and demonstrated high rates of surgical down-staging or avoidance.
- **Hypercalcaemia of Malignancy**: A single-arm study in patients refractory to bisphosphonates showed 63.6% achieved a response (CSC ≤ 11.5 mg/dL) by Day 10.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C.
- **Handling**: Keep in the original carton to protect from light. Do not freeze. Do not shake.
- **Excursion**: Once removed from the refrigerator, Wyost may be kept at room temperature (up to 25°C) but must be used within 30 days.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule/Legal Classification**:
    - **Australian**: Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
    - **US**: Rx-only.
