# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA).
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the official Product Information for Levocarnitine Injection. It is designed as a clinically accurate reference for healthcare professionals to facilitate the safe and effective use of the medication.
*   **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical guide; it does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN).
*   **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative ((R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt).
*   **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
*   **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    *   Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
*   **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
*   **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
*   **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    *   **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
    *   **Initial Management:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. Administration should occur every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
    *   **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically in the range of 50 mg/kg, though higher doses may be required. The maximum recorded dose is 300 mg/kg [US].
*   **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    *   **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
    *   **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    *   **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels. Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if levels are adequate [US].

### Administration Instructions
*   Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
*   Levocarnitine is compatible with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s solution for infusion [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, therapy must be discontinued immediately and appropriate medical management initiated [US].
*   **Neurologic Effects (Seizures):** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a known seizure history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
*   **Monitoring Requirements:**
    *   **Baseline:** Obtain plasma carnitine concentrations before initiating parenteral therapy [US].
    *   **Routine:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels is recommended.
    *   **Therapeutic Target:** Plasma free carnitine concentrations should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect frequencies are derived from clinical trials in ESRD patients [US].

*   **Very Common (>10%):**
    *   **General:** Injection site reaction (27–38%), pain (21–35%), headache (3–37%), chest pain (6–15%), flu syndrome (15–29%).
    *   **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%).
    *   **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), abdominal pain (5–21%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%).
    *   **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%), rhinitis (6–11%).
    *   **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (6–15%), hypervolemia (3–12%).

*   **Common (1–10%):**
    *   **General:** Asthenia, fever, infection, back pain, accidental injury.
    *   **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia, palpitations, arrhythmia, atrial fibrillation.
    *   **Digestive:** Anorexia, constipation, dyspepsia, melena.
    *   **Metabolic:** Hyperkalemia, peripheral edema, weight changes.
    *   **Nervous System:** Dizziness, paresthesia, insomnia, depression, anxiety.
    *   **Skin:** Pruritus, rash.
    *   **Special Senses:** Amblyopia, eye disorder, taste perversion.

*   **Rare/Post-marketing (<0.1% or unspecified):**
    *   Body odor, gastritis, seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
*   **Management:** It is recommended to monitor INR levels frequently in patients receiving warfarin therapy after starting levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** 
    *   **US Category B (Equivalent):** Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. However, there are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine levels following exogenous administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
*   **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal reproductive studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for paediatric patients with inborn errors of metabolism is consistent with adult weight-based dosing (50 mg/kg) [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the available source; general dosing principles apply.
*   **Renal Impairment:** 
    *   Levocarnitine is indicated for use in ESRD patients on hemodialysis.
    *   Oral levocarnitine safety has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency; chronic high doses of oral formulations in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for beta-oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
*   **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with metabolic defects or ESRD, levocarnitine helps clear accumulated toxic acyl-CoA compounds by forming acylcarnitine, which is then excreted.
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    *   **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and [³H]-γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    *   **Elimination:** Following intravenous administration, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **ESRD Study:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg levocarnitine three times weekly after dialysis showed an increase in mean plasma levocarnitine concentrations from 20 µmol/L (pre-dialysis baseline) to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks of therapy [US].
*   **Clinical Outcomes:** While levocarnitine injection effectively increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the impact on specific clinical outcomes or signs and symptoms of carnitine deficiency in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
*   **Conditions:** Avoid excessive heat and protect from freezing.
*   **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial, as the formulation contains no preservatives.
*   **Stability:** When diluted in Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's (0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL), the solution is stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags [US].
