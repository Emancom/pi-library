# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA). 
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the official Product Information (PI) for **levocarnitine** injection. It is intended as a concise reference for healthcare professionals to aid clinical decision-making.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial summary and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for the most current and region-specific prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule involved in the transport of long-chain fatty acids.
- **Chemical Name:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Clear, colorless sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing hemodialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - **Administration:** Delivered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion. 
    - **Initial Phase:** Patients in severe metabolic crisis may require a loading dose, followed by equivalent doses administered every 3 or 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours).
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically remain at 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised based on clinical requirements.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - **Timing:** Administered as a slow (2 to 3 minute) bolus into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Adjustments:** Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations.

### Monitoring and Administration Instructions
- **Target Concentrations:** It is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Monitoring should target a plasma free carnitine concentration between 35 and 60 µmol/L.
- **Visual Inspection:** Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration before administration.
- **Stability:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** No contraindications were identified in the available source document beyond general hypersensitivity considerations [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred, predominantly in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. Treatment must be discontinued immediately if severe reactions occur [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known epilepsy, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulation Caution):** While the injection is used in ESRD, the safety of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severe renal impairment has not been established. Chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common and Serious Reactions
- **Very Common (>10%):** In ESRD clinical trials, common events included transient nausea, vomiting, hypertension (18-21%), diarrhea (9-35%), headache (3-37%), and injection site reactions (27-38%).
- **Other Reactions:** Body odor and gastritis have been noted. 
- **Cardiovascular:** Arrhythmia, atrial fibrillation, palpitations, and tachycardia have been reported at varying frequencies in dialysis patients [US].
- **Neurological:** Seizures (post-marketing report).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. Frequent INR monitoring is required following initiation or dose adjustment of levocarnitine in patients on anticoagulant therapy [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Levocarnitine does not bind to plasma proteins or albumin, reducing the likelihood of protein-binding displacement interactions [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned by letter, but the PI states the drug should be used only if clearly needed]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm.
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased milk concentrations of levocarnitine following exogenous administration. Risks to the infant from excess carnitine intake must be weighed against maternal benefit [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is based on mg/kg, applicable to pediatric populations with inborn errors of metabolism [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific dose adjustments for the elderly are not detailed in the available source [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Used specifically in ESRD patients on dialysis (see Dosage and Administration). Caution is advised regarding the accumulation of metabolites if oral formulations are used in this population [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Efficacy:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly showed that mean pre-dialysis trough concentrations increased from 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L at baseline to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks of therapy. While levocarnitine successfully increases plasma concentrations, the definitive impact on clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been fully determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat and protect from freezing.
- **Handling:** Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].
