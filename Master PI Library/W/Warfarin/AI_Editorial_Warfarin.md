# Editorial Product Information: Warfarin Sodium

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of warfarin sodium for healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, dosing strategies, and safety profiles.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, legally binding Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the Australian PI was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Warfarin sodium (a vitamin K dependent factor anticoagulant; racemic mixture).
- **Chemical Class:** Coumarin derivative [4-hydroxy-3-(3-oxo-1-phenylbutyl) coumarin].
- **Brand Names:** **Coumadin** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** B01AA03 (Vitamin K antagonist).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Oral tablets: 1 mg (light tan), 2 mg (lavender), 5 mg (green) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Viatris Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Based on available sources, warfarin is indicated for:
- Prophylaxis and/or treatment of venous thrombosis and its extension, and pulmonary embolism [Australian].
- Prophylaxis and/or treatment of thromboembolic complications associated with atrial fibrillation [Australian].
- **Note:** Not indicated for "lone" atrial fibrillation in patients <60 years of age with no other risk factors (e.g., prior thromboembolism, diabetes, hypertension) [Australian].
- Adjunctive treatment in coronary occlusion [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing Principles
Warfarin is a narrow therapeutic index (NTI) drug. Treatment must be highly individualised based on the patient's International Normalised Ratio (INR) response and clinical condition.

### Initial Dosage
- **Standard Induction:** 10 mg daily for 2 to 4 days, with subsequent adjustments based on daily PT/INR results [Australian].
- **Low-Dose Induction:** Recommended for elderly, debilitated patients, or those with known sensitivity [Australian].
- **Note:** Large loading doses (e.g., 30 mg) are not recommended as they increase haemorrhage risk without providing more rapid protection [Australian].

### Maintenance Dosing
- Most patients are maintained on 2 mg to 10 mg daily [Australian].
- Individual doses should be adjusted to maintain the target INR.

### Target INR Ranges [Australian Recommended]
- **2.0–2.5:** Prophylaxis of deep-vein thrombosis (DVT) including high-risk surgery.
- **2.0–3.0:** Treatment of DVT, pulmonary embolism, and atrial fibrillation.
- **2.5–3.5:** Recurrent DVT and PE; arterial disease (including myocardial infarction); arterial grafts; prosthetic heart valves.

### Conversion from Other Anticoagulants
- **Heparin:** Overlap therapy for 3 to 6 days is common due to warfarin's delayed onset. Discontinue heparin only when the desired INR range is achieved [Australian].
- **NOACs/DOACs:** Transitioning is considered a high-risk situation; specific switching protocols in the NOAC's PI should be consulted [Australian].

### Missed Dose Instructions
- Take the missed dose as soon as possible on the same day. Do **not** double the dose the next day to make up for a missed dose [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
Warfarin is contraindicated in the following scenarios [Australian]:
- Any condition where the hazard of haemorrhage outweighs the potential clinical benefit.
- Haemorrhagic tendencies or blood dyscrasias.
- Recent or contemplated surgery of the central nervous system (CNS) or eye.
- Traumatic surgery resulting in large open surfaces.
- Active ulceration or overt bleeding (GI, GU, or respiratory tracts).
- Cerebrovascular haemorrhage.
- Aneurysms (cerebral or dissecting aorta).
- Pericarditis and pericardial effusions.
- Bacterial endocarditis.
- **Pregnancy:** Known to cause fatal fetal haemorrhage and malformations.
- Threatened abortion, eclampsia, and preeclampsia.
- Lack of patient cooperation (e.g., unsupervised senility, alcoholism, psychosis).
- Inadequate laboratory facilities for monitoring.
- Spinal puncture and procedures with potential for uncontrollable bleeding.
- Major regional or lumbar block anaesthesia.
- Malignant hypertension.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Narrow Therapeutic Index:** Small changes in dose or external factors (diet, drugs) can lead to significant INR fluctuations [Australian].
- **Haemorrhage:** The most serious risk; may occur in any tissue or organ. Risk increases significantly with INR >4.0 [Australian].
- **Tissue Necrosis/Gangrene:** Rare (<0.1%); usually associated with local thrombosis within days of starting therapy. Often linked to Protein C or S deficiency [Australian].
- **Anticoagulant-Related Nephropathy (ARN):** Acute kidney injury may occur, often associated with over-anticoagulation and haematuria [Australian].
- **Systemic Atheroemboli (including "Purple Toes Syndrome"):** May occur when warfarin releases cholesterol microemboli from plaques. Discontinue if observed [Australian].
- **Calciphylaxis:** Rare vascular calcification with cutaneous necrosis; carries high mortality. More common in end-stage renal disease but reported in patients with normal renal function [Australian].
- **Interchangeability:** Do **not** interchange different brands (e.g., Coumadin and Marevan) as bioequivalence has not been established [Australian].

### Monitoring
- Periodic determination of PT/INR is essential.
- Once stable, intervals between tests are normally 1 to 4 weeks [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common/Serious** | Haemorrhage (fatal or non-fatal) from any tissue/organ [Australian]. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Skin/tissue necrosis, gangrene (breast, penis, limbs) [Australian]. |
| **Not Known/Infrequent** | Anticoagulant-related nephropathy, haematuria, purple toes syndrome, vasculitis, hepatitis, cholestatic liver injury, jaundice, elevated liver enzymes [Australian]. |
| **Infrequent** | Hypersensitivity, alopecia, rash, dermatitis, urticaria, nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, abdominal pain, flatulence, headache, dizziness, taste perversion, paraesthesia, chills, fever [Australian]. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
Warfarin is subject to a vast number of interactions. Frequent INR monitoring is required when adding or deleting any medication [Australian].

### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP2C9, 1A2, 3A4 Inhibitors:** (e.g., Amiodarone, Fluconazole, Metronidazole, Ciprofloxacin) Increase INR and bleeding risk [Australian].
- **CYP2C9, 1A2, 3A4 Inducers:** (e.g., Rifampicin, Carbamazepine, St John’s Wort, Smoking) Decrease INR and increase thromboembolic risk [Australian].

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Increased Bleeding Risk:** NSAIDs (including aspirin), antiplatelet agents (clopidogrel), SSRIs/SNRIs, and other anticoagulants [Australian].

### Dietary and Herbal Interactions
- **Vitamin K:** High dietary intake (green leafy vegetables) decreases warfarin's effect [Australian].
- **Botanicals:** Garlic and Ginkgo may increase bleeding risk; Co-enzyme Q10 and Ginseng may decrease warfarin's effect [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Contraindicated. Warfarin crosses the placenta. Exposure during the first trimester can cause "fetal warfarin syndrome" (nasal hypoplasia, stippled epiphyses). CNS abnormalities and fetal death can occur in any trimester [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Warfarin appears in breast milk in an **inactive form**. No change in PT was observed in nursed infants. It is generally considered safe, though premature infants have not been evaluated [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Reproductive effects have not been evaluated in humans [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children <18 years have not been established in RCTs; however, use is well-documented. More frequent INR monitoring is required due to dosing difficulties [Australian].
- **Elderly:** Patients ≥60 years exhibit greater than expected INR responses. Lower maintenance doses are usually required [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Renal clearance is a minor determinant of response. No specific dose adjustment is necessary for renal failure, but close monitoring for ARN is advised [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** May potentiate warfarin effect due to impaired synthesis of clotting factors and decreased metabolism [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits the VKORC1 enzyme complex, preventing the regeneration of vitamin K1 epoxide. This interferes with the synthesis of vitamin K-dependent clotting factors (II, VII, IX, X) [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Completely absorbed; peak concentration within 4 hours [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** 99% bound to plasma albumin. Small volume of distribution (0.14 L/kg) [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Stereoselectively metabolised by CYP450 enzymes (S-enantiomer via CYP2C9; R-enantiomer via CYP1A2 and 3A4) [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Effective half-life is approximately 40 hours. Up to 92% is excreted in urine, primarily as metabolites [Australian].
