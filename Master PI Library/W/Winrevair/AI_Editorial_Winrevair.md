# Editorial Product Information: Winrevair (Sotatercept)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis harmonises Product Information (PI) documents from the following jurisdictions:
    - **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**
    - **US (Food and Drug Administration)**
- **Purpose:** This document provides a unified, clinically accurate overview of **Winrevair** for healthcare professionals, integrating global regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis for informational purposes and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Sotatercept (generic name); Sotatercept-csrk [US].
- **Chemical Class:** Activin signalling inhibitor; recombinant human homodimeric fusion protein (ActRIIA linked to human IgG1 Fc domain).
- **Brand Name:** **Winrevair**
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile, preservative-free, white to off-white lyophilised powder for subcutaneous injection.
    - 45 mg single-dose vial (reconstituted to 50 mg/mL; deliverable volume 0.9 mL).
    - 60 mg single-dose vial (reconstituted to 50 mg/mL; deliverable volume 1.2 mL).
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Merck Sharp & Dohme.

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension (PAH):** Treatment of adults with PAH (WHO Group 1 [US]; WHO Functional Class II, III, or IV [Australian]) to improve exercise capacity, improve WHO functional class, and reduce the risk of clinical worsening (including hospitalisation, lung transplantation, and death).
- **Clinical Context:** Efficacy is established in idiopathic PAH, heritable PAH, PAH associated with connective tissue disease, drug- or toxin-induced PAH, and PAH associated with congenital heart disease with repaired shunts [Australian].
- **Usage:** Typically used in combination with standard PAH therapy.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Starting Dose:** 0.3 mg/kg administered once every 3 weeks (Q3W).
- **Target Dose:** 0.7 mg/kg once every 3 weeks (Q3W).
- **Dose Escalation:** After the initial 0.3 mg/kg dose, the dose should be increased to the 0.7 mg/kg target dose at the next scheduled visit (Week 3), provided haemoglobin (Hgb) and platelet counts are within acceptable ranges.

### Administration Instructions
- **Route:** Subcutaneous injection into the abdomen, upper thigh, or upper arm. 
- **Site Selection:** Avoid areas that are scarred, tender, or bruised. For patient/caregiver self-administration, only the abdomen and upper thigh are recommended.
- **Reconstitution:** Must be reconstituted with the provided Sterile Water for Injection. Gently swirl; **do not shake**. Reconstituted solution should be clear to opalescent and colorless to slightly brownish-yellow.
- **Dose Calculation:** 
    - Injection Volume (mL) = [Patient Weight (kg) × Dose (mg/kg)] ÷ 50 mg/mL.
    - Volume should be rounded to the nearest 0.1 mL.
- **Multiple Vials:** If the required dose requires two vials (45 mg + 45 mg or 60 mg + 60 mg), a 2-vial administration pack should be used to combine the contents into a single syringe.

### Missed Dose
- Administer as soon as possible. 
- If the dose is missed by >3 days, adjust the schedule to maintain the 3-week interval from the date of the late dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to sotatercept or any of its excipients [Australian]. 
- **US Note:** The US PI lists "None."

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Erythrocytosis:** Significant increases in Hgb have been observed. Severe erythrocytosis may increase the risk of thromboembolic events or hyperviscosity syndrome. 
    - **Monitoring:** Obtain Hgb and platelet counts prior to the first dose, before each of the first 5 doses, and periodically thereafter.
    - **Dose Delay Thresholds:** Delay treatment for at least 3 weeks if:
        - Hgb increases >2.0 g/dL from the previous dose and is above the upper limit of normal (ULN).
        - Hgb increases >4.0 g/dL from baseline.
        - Hgb increases >2.0 g/dL above ULN.
- **Severe Thrombocytopenia:** Decreased platelet counts can occur, particularly in patients also receiving prostacyclin infusions. 
    - **Initiation Threshold:** Do not initiate if platelets are <50,000/mm³.
    - **Dose Delay:** Delay treatment for at least 3 weeks if platelets decrease to <50,000/mm³.
- **Serious Bleeding:** Reported in 4% (STELLAR trial) to 7% (ZENITH trial) of patients. Risk is higher with concomitant prostacyclin therapy, antithrombotic agents, or low platelet counts. Do not administer if the patient is experiencing a serious active bleed.
- **Embryofetal Toxicity:** May cause fetal harm based on animal data.
- **Impaired Fertility:** Animal studies suggest potential for impaired male and female fertility.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect frequency and severity were higher in patients with more advanced disease (ZENITH trial) compared to the pivotal STELLAR trial.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Headache (20–24%), Epistaxis (12–45%), Telangiectasia (10–25%), Diarrhea (12–25%), Dizziness (10–15%), Erythema (10–13%), Rash (5–20%), Infections [US] |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Nausea, Fatigue, Injection site pain, Thrombocytopenia (6%), Dyspnoea, Hypokalaemia, Gingival bleeding, Increased Blood Pressure |
| **Uncommon (<1%)** | Intrapulmonary right-to-left shunting (reported in SOTERIA study), Pericardial effusion (post-marketing) |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Formal Studies:** No clinical drug interaction studies have been performed.
- **Metabolism:** Sotatercept is a protein and is expected to undergo catabolism into small peptides; it is not metabolised by cytochrome P450 enzymes.
- **Management:** Monitor Hgb and platelets closely when used with other agents that affect these parameters or those that increase bleeding risk (e.g., prostacyclins, anticoagulants).

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category D:** Drugs which have caused, are suspected to have caused or may be expected to cause, an increased incidence of human fetal malformations or irreversible damage.
    - **US Recommendation:** Advise females of reproductive potential to use effective contraception during treatment and for **4 months** after the final dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety is not established. Breastfeeding is **not recommended** during treatment and for 4 months after the final dose.
- **Fertility:** Animal data showed decreased pregnancy rates and histologic changes in reproductive organs (some irreversible in males).

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients <18 years of age.
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment required. Bleeding events occurred more frequently in the older subgroup [Australian]. US PI notes insufficient data for patients ≥75 years.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment required for mild to moderate impairment. Limited data available for severe impairment (eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73m²). Not dialysable.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not studied in patients with hepatic impairment. It is not expected to affect metabolism as the drug undergoes cellular catabolism.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** An activin signalling inhibitor. It binds to activin A and other TGF-β superfamily ligands, restoring the balance between pro-proliferative (ActRIIA/Smad2/3) and anti-proliferative (BMPRII/Smad1/5/8) signalling. This inhibits vascular cell proliferation and improves haemodynamics.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is ~66%. $T_{max}$ is ~7 days.
    - **Distribution:** Central volume of distribution ($V_c$) is ~3.6 L.
    - **Elimination:** Effective half-life is approximately 21–24 days. Clearance is ~0.18 L/day.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Reduces pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR) and NT-proBNP levels.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **STELLAR Trial:** A global, double-blind study of 323 patients (WHO FC II or III). Winrevair (0.7 mg/kg) plus standard care resulted in a 40.8-meter placebo-adjusted median increase in 6-Minute Walk Distance (6MWD) at Week 24 ($p < 0.001$). There was an 84% reduction in the risk of death or PAH clinical worsening events compared to placebo.
- **ZENITH Trial:** Evaluated 172 patients at high risk of mortality (WHO FC III or IV). Winrevair resulted in a 76% lower risk of a first event of all-cause death, lung transplant, or PAH-related hospitalisation (HR: 0.24).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C.
- **Protection:** Keep in the original carton to protect from light.
- **Stability:** Reconstituted solution should be used immediately or within **4 hours** at room temperature.
- **Excursions:** Unused kits can be out of the refrigerator (up to 25°C) for up to 24 hours [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Additional Monitoring:** Subject to additional monitoring (Black Triangle symbol) [Australian].
