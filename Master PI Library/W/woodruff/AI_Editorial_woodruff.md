# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the official Product Information for levocarnitine injection. It is designed as a concise, clinically accurate reference for healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial summary and does not replace the official Product Information approved in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI when prescribing or administering this medication.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic management of patients with metabolic defects resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients requiring maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Administration:** Administer via slow intravenous bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to establish baseline plasma carnitine levels and perform weekly/monthly monitoring (blood chemistry, vital signs, plasma concentrations). The target plasma free carnitine concentration is 35 to 60 µmol/L [US].

### Indication-Specific Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose may be followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours (administered every 3 or 4 hours, and no less frequently than every 6 hours). Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight). Administer into the venous return line after each dialysis session. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) should be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine levels (normal range 40 to 50 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known:** No specific contraindications are identified in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These typically occur in ESRD patients and may manifest within minutes of administration. Treatment should be discontinued immediately upon a severe reaction [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Levocarnitine may increase the frequency or severity of seizures in patients with pre-existing seizure disorders. Seizures have also been reported in patients without a prior history of epilepsy [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral vs. IV):** The safety of high-dose **oral** levocarnitine has not been established in patients with severe renal impairment or ESRD, as chronic oral use may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) normally excreted in urine [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse event data from clinical trials in dialysis patients indicate a high background of symptoms related to the underlying pathology.
- **Very Common (>10%):** Injection site reaction (33–59%), pain (30–49%), hypertension (14–21%), headache (3–37%), pharyngitis (15–33%), nausea (5–12%), vomiting (9–21%), and diarrhea (9–35%) [US].
- **Common (1–10%):** Chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, asthenia, abdominal pain, dizziness, insomnia, and palpitations [US].
- **Other Reported Effects:** Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequently, body odor and gastritis.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis and seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Concurrent use has been associated with reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR). 
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin anticoagulation [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Not formally categorised by letter, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm.] Use during pregnancy only if clearly required [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk; studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations following exogenous administration. The risks of excess intake to the infant must be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
- **Fertility:** No mutagenic effects were observed in standard tests ($Salmonella$, $Saccharomyces$, $Schizosaccharomyces$); long-term carcinogenicity studies have not been conducted [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy are established for metabolic disorders and ESRD; dosing is weight-based (see Section 3) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosing adjustments for ESRD patients are provided based on post-dialysis administration (see Section 3).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, facilitating beta-oxidation and energy production in tissues (excluding the brain) [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with metabolic defects or ESRD, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly eliminated [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (faeces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis trough concentrations from approximately 20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L. While it effectively increases carnitine levels, the specific impact on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, arrhythmias) in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion as the product contains no preservatives. Inspect visually for particulates or discoloration before use [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Prescription only (Rx-only).
