# Editorial Product Information: Wainua (eplontersen)

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information documents contributed to this editorial: United States (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis intended for use by healthcare professionals. It provides a structured clinical overview of **eplontersen**.
- **Note:** This editorial synthesis is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labelling for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Eplontersen (as eplontersen sodium). 
- **Chemical Class:** Transthyretin-directed antisense oligonucleotide (ASO) covalently linked to a ligand containing three N-acetyl galactosamine (GalNAc) residues.
- **Brand Name:** **Wainua** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Injection: 45 mg/0.8 mL in a single-dose autoinjector [US].
    - Injection: 45 mg/0.8 mL in a single-dose prefilled syringe [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AstraZeneca Pharmaceuticals LP [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Polyneuropathy of hereditary transthyretin-mediated amyloidosis (hATTR):** Indicated for the treatment of polyneuropathy caused by hATTR in adults [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The recommended dose is 45 mg administered once monthly via subcutaneous injection [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Inject into the abdomen or upper thigh. 
    - The back of the upper arm may be used if administered by a healthcare professional or caregiver.
    - Rotate injection sites with each dose.
    - Do not inject within 5 cm (2 inches) of the navel or into areas of skin that are tender, bruised, red, hard, scaly, or scarred.
    - **Prefilled Syringe:** Must be administered only by a healthcare professional [US].
    - **Autoinjector:** Intended for self-administration or administration by a caregiver after proper training [US].
- **Missed Dose Instructions:** Administer the missed dose as soon as possible. Dosing should then be resumed at monthly intervals from the date of the most recently administered dose [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None:** There are no contraindications listed in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
- **Reduced Serum Vitamin A and Supplementation:**
    - **Wainua** treatment leads to a reduction in serum vitamin A levels. 
    - Patients should be advised to take the recommended daily allowance (RDA) of vitamin A.
    - Higher-than-RDA doses should not be used to attempt to normalise serum vitamin A levels, as these levels do not accurately reflect total body vitamin A stores.
    - Refer patients to an ophthalmologist if they develop ocular symptoms suggestive of vitamin A deficiency (e.g., dry eyes, night blindness) [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:**
    - Monitor for ocular symptoms of vitamin A deficiency.
    - Serum vitamin A levels should be understood as inherently lowered by the mechanism of the drug [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions observed in clinical trials (at least 5% frequency) are summarised below:

| Adverse Reaction | Frequency (%) [US] |
| :--- | :--- |
| Vitamin A decreased* | 15% |
| Vomiting | 9% |
| Proteinuria | 8% |
| Injection site reactions† | 7% |
| Blurred vision | 6% |
| Cataract | 6% |

*\*Includes vitamin A deficiency and decreased serum levels.*  
*†Includes pain, erythema, and pruritus.*

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Atrioventricular (AV) heart block was reported in 2% of patients, including one case of complete AV block [US].
- **Immunogenicity:** Treatment-emergent anti-drug antibodies (ADAs) occurred in 37% of patients. While ADAs increased the trough concentration ($C_{trough}$), they did not appear to impact eplontersen’s $C_{max}$, AUC, safety, or efficacy based on limited data [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No clinical drug-drug interaction studies have been conducted. In vitro data indicate that eplontersen is not a substrate, inhibitor, or inducer of major cytochrome P450 (CYP) enzymes or transporters.
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Interactions with highly plasma protein-bound drugs are not expected [US].
- **Management:** No specific management notes for drug interactions are provided in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not assigned (PLLR format). 
    - There are no available data on use in pregnant women. 
    - **Clinical Considerations:** Treatment reduces serum vitamin A levels, which are essential for normal fetal development. Both deficiency and excess of vitamin A are associated with adverse outcomes. 
    - **Animal Data:** No adverse developmental effects were observed in mice at doses up to 75 mg/kg [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There is no information regarding the presence of eplontersen in human milk or its effects on milk production or the breastfed infant. The developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the clinical need for the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in mice showed no adverse effects on male or female fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in paediatric patients have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use ($\ge$65 years):** No dose adjustment is required. Clinical studies included 31% of patients aged 65–74 and 5.6% aged $\ge$75. No overall differences in safety/efficacy were observed, though greater sensitivity in some older individuals cannot be ruled out [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for patients with mild to moderate renal impairment (eGFR 30 to <90 mL/min/1.73 $m^2$). It has not been studied in patients with severe renal impairment or end-stage renal disease [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for patients with mild hepatic impairment. It has not been studied in patients with moderate or severe hepatic impairment, or in those with a prior liver transplant [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Eplontersen is an antisense oligonucleotide (ASO)-GalNAc conjugate. It binds to transthyretin (TTR) mRNA, leading to its degradation. This results in reduced serum TTR protein and reduced TTR protein deposits in tissues [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** After monthly dosing, mean serum TTR was reduced by 81% from baseline by Week 35. Serum vitamin A was reduced by 71% by Week 37 [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** $T_{max}$ is approximately 2 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Distributes primarily to the liver and kidney cortex. Plasma protein binding is >98%.
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolised by endo- and exonucleases to short oligonucleotide fragments.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 3 weeks. Less than 1% of the dose is excreted unchanged in urine within 24 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study 1 (NEURO-TTRansform):** A randomized, open-label, multicenter trial in 144 adults with hATTR polyneuropathy. The study compared **Wainua** (45 mg monthly) to an external placebo group.
- **Primary Outcomes:**
    - **mNIS+7:** Significant improvement in the modified Neuropathy Impairment Scale+7 compared to placebo (LS mean change: 0.2 vs 9.2; p < 0.001).
    - **Norfolk QoL-DN:** Significant improvement in the Norfolk Quality of Life-Diabetic Neuropathy total score compared to placebo (LS mean change: -3.1 vs 8.7; p < 0.001) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Refrigerated Storage:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F) in the original carton to protect from light [US].
- **Room Temperature Excursion:** May be kept at room temperature (up to 30°C/86°F) in the original carton for up to 6 weeks. If not used within 6 weeks, the product must be discarded.
- **Special Handling:** Do not freeze. Do not expose to heat [US].
