# Editorial Product Information: Non-Standardized Allergenic Extracts (Western Wheatgrass)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**. 
- Information for other regions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian, Japanese) was not provided for this review. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- This synthesis serves as a clinical reference and is not a substitute for the official PI used for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Non-standardised allergenic extracts. Specifically identified in this context: **Western Wheatgrass** (*Pascopyrum smithii*) pollen [US].
- **Chemical Class**: Allergenic extract.
- **Brand Names**: Provided by Greer Laboratories, Inc. [US].
- **ATC Code**: V01AA02 (Grass pollens).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s)**: 
    - Sterile aqueous stock concentrates: Up to 1:10 weight/volume (w/v) or 40,000 Protein Nitrogen Units (PNU)/mL [US].
    - 50% Glycerin stock concentrates: Up to 1:20 w/v or 40,000 PNU/mL [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration**: Percutaneous (prick or puncture), intradermal, and subcutaneous [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Greer Laboratories, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Diagnostic Testing**: Indicated for the skin test diagnosis of patients exhibiting a clinical history of allergies to specific allergens [US].
- **Immunotherapy**: Indicated for the reduction of allergen-induced symptoms in patients with confirmed sensitivity (via positive skin tests or in vitro IgE testing) [US].
- **Limitation of Use**: Food extracts included in this product class are not proven safe or effective for immunotherapy and are indicated for diagnostic use only [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- Dosages are highly individualised and must be adjusted based on patient response and sensitivity. 
- **Preparation**: Parenteral products must be inspected for particulates or discoloration. Stock concentrates require dilution with sterile diluents (e.g., normal saline, buffered saline, or HSA-saline) [US].

### Diagnostic Testing
- **Percutaneous (Prick/Puncture)**: Typically uses undiluted 50% glycerin stock concentrates. If high sensitivity is suspected, 5-fold or 10-fold dilutions should be used [US].
- **Intradermal**: Uses much lower concentrations (dilutions of 1:100 to 1:1000 v/v). If negative, stronger doses may be used up to 1:25 v/v [US].

### Immunotherapy (Subcutaneous Only)
- **Build-up Phase**: Injections are generally administered 1–2 times per week. The initial dose is based on skin reactivity (e.g., 0.05 mL of a highly dilute extract for high-risk patients) [US].
- **Dose Increments**: Doses are increased by no more than 50% of the previous dose, provided the last injection was well-tolerated [US].
- **Maintenance Phase**: Once the maximum tolerated dose is reached, the interval between injections is increased to every 2, 3, and finally 4 weeks [US].
- **Duration**: The typical course of treatment is 3–5 years [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Administer subcutaneously in the lateral aspect of the upper arm or thigh. Avoid intravascular injection [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Asthma**: Patients with severe, unstable, or uncontrolled asthma [US].
- **Prior Reactions**: History of any severe systemic or local allergic reaction to an allergen extract [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning [US]
- **Severe Allergic Reactions**: Non-standardised allergenic extracts can cause life-threatening systemic reactions, including anaphylaxis. Administration must occur in a healthcare setting under the supervision of a physician prepared to manage emergencies. Patients must be observed for at least 30 minutes post-administration.

### General Warnings
- **Beta-Blockers**: Patients taking beta-blockers may be unresponsive to epinephrine and are at higher risk if a systemic reaction occurs [US].
- **High-Risk Situations**: Increased risk of systemic reactions is associated with extreme sensitivity, high doses, accelerated "rush" schedules, or switching between different manufacturer lots [US].
- **False Negatives**: Specifically for food extracts, false-negative skin tests can occur. If history suggests allergy, confirm with serology (IgE) or oral food challenge [US].

### Monitoring
- Evaluate the patient for symptoms of rhinitis, asthma, or infection prior to each injection, as these may require dose reduction or withholding [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Local Reactions (Very Common, 26–82%)**: Erythema, pruritus, swelling, tenderness, and pain at the injection site. Local reactions persisting >24 hours require dose adjustment [US].
- **Systemic Reactions (Common, $\le$7%)**: Generalized urticaria, pruritus, angioedema, rhinitis, wheezing, laryngeal edema, and hypotension. These reactions can be fatal [US].
- **Skin Discoloration**: Extracts containing sodium formaldehyde sulfoxylate (SFS) may cause long-term skin discoloration at the site of diagnostic administration [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Antihistamines**: First-generation (e.g., diphenhydramine) and second-generation (e.g., cetirizine) antihistamines suppress skin reactivity. Testing should be delayed for 3–10 days after discontinuation [US].
- **Topical Corticosteroids/Anesthetics**: Can suppress skin reactivity or flare responses; discontinue use at the test site for 2–3 weeks prior to testing [US].
- **Tricyclic Antidepressants**: Possess potent antihistamine activity; testing should be delayed for 7–14 days after discontinuation [US].
- **Epinephrine Potentiators**: Levothyroxine, MAOIs, and certain antihistamines may increase the adverse effects of epinephrine if used to treat a reaction [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: No human or animal data are available to establish risk. Use during pregnancy should be carefully weighed against potential benefits [US].
- **Breastfeeding**: It is not known if extracts are excreted in human milk. Consideration should be given to the developmental benefits of breastfeeding and the clinical necessity for the mother [US].
- **Fertility**: Data not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Safety and efficacy in children under 5 years of age require caution due to potential difficulty in communicating symptoms of systemic reactions and cooperation with the regimen [US].
- **Elderly Use**: Clinical data are insufficient to determine if patients aged 65 and older respond differently than younger subjects [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment**: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**:
    - **Diagnostic**: Interaction between the introduced allergen and IgE bound to mast cells leads to degranulation and release of histamine/mediators, producing a "wheal and flare" [US].
    - **Immunotherapy**: The exact mechanism is unknown, but treatment is associated with shifts in IgE/IgG levels and regulatory T-cell responses [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics/Pharmacodynamics**: Not described in terms of standard ADME profiles; clinical effect is mediated through local immune system interaction [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Specific pivotal trial data for Western Wheatgrass were not detailed in the source document. The PI notes that the efficacy of specific immunotherapy with allergenic extracts has been reviewed by the FDA advisory panel and found helpful in reducing symptoms associated with offending allergens [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Maintain between 2°C and 8°C (36°F to 46°F) at all times [US].
- **Stability**: 
    - Dilutions in <50% glycerin have reduced stability.
    - 1:100 v/v dilutions should be kept no longer than one month.
    - More dilute solutions should be kept no longer than one week [US].
