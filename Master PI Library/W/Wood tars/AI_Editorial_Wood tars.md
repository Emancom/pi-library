## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine ((R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Levocarnitine** Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Administer via the intravenous route only. 
- For parenteral products, inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.

### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg.
- **Method:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Should be administered every 3 or 4 hours; frequency should never be less than every 6 hours.
- **Maximum Dose:** Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered.

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
- **Method:** Administer as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma **levocarnitine** concentrations. Adjustments (e.g., downward to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy.

### Monitoring
- **Target Plasma Levels:** For metabolic disorders, free carnitine concentrations should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L.
- **ESRD Troughs:** Normal pre-dialysis levels are typically 40 µmol/L to 50 µmol/L.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions can occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue immediately and initiate medical treatment.
- **Neurologic Effects (Seizures):** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed.

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Oral Formulation in Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of *oral* **levocarnitine** have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted in urine.
- **Monitoring Requirements:** In metabolic disorders, monitoring should include blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of the patient population (ESRD or metabolic disorders).

| Frequency | Adverse Event [US Data] |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Injection site reaction (33–38%), Pharyngitis (15–27%), Hypertension (18–21%), Diarrhea (9–35%), Headache (3–37%), Fever (5–12%), Chest pain (6–15%), Flu syndrome (15–29%), Infection (10–24%), Pain (21–35%) |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, and laryngeal edema.
- **Postmarketing Experience:** Neurologic reactions (seizures) have been identified during post-approval use.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with **levocarnitine**. 
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels in patients on warfarin therapy after initiating **levocarnitine** or following dose adjustments.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate/controlled studies exist in pregnant women. Use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased milk concentrations of carnitine following administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefits to the mother. Discontinuation of nursing or treatment may be considered [US].
- **Fertility:** Mutagenicity tests (Salmonella, Saccharomyces, Schizosaccharomyces) were negative. Long-term animal carcinogenicity studies have not been performed.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD is weight-based (see Section 3).
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for use in ESRD patients on hemodialysis. Note the precaution regarding the accumulation of metabolites (TMA/TMAO) if using oral formulations in this population.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Levocarnitine** is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism or ESRD, **levocarnitine** promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly eliminated.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; the mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In two double-blind, placebo-controlled trials of ESRD patients on chronic hemodialysis, **levocarnitine** was shown to increase plasma carnitine concentrations. However, the effects of this supplementation on specific clinical outcomes in the ESRD population (beyond biochemical correction) have not been fully determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F).
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC plastic bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer's (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) at room temperature.
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives.
