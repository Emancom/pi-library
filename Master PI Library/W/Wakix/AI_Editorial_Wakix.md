# Editorial Product Information: Pitolisant

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of pitolisant product information for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesizes regulatory data into a single, clinically focused reference.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labeling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Pitolisant (as hydrochloride).
- **Chemical Class:** Histamine-3 (H3) receptor antagonist/inverse agonist.
- **Brand Name(s):** **WAKIX** [US].
- **ATC Code:** N07XX11 (Note: Not explicitly stated in the source PI but standard for this class).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
  - Film-coated tablets: 4.45 mg (white, round, biconvex, marked with "S") and 17.8 mg (white, round, biconvex, marked with "H") [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Harmony Biosciences, LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Narcolepsy:** Treatment of excessive daytime sleepiness (EDS) or cataplexy in patients 6 years of age and older [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Administration:** Administer once daily in the morning immediately upon awakening.
- **Titration:** It may take up to 8 weeks to achieve a full clinical response.

### Adult Dosage [US]
- **Week 1:** 8.9 mg (two 4.45 mg tablets) once daily.
- **Week 2:** Increase to 17.8 mg (one 17.8 mg tablet) once daily.
- **Week 3:** May increase to a maximum of 35.6 mg (two 17.8 mg tablets) once daily based on response and tolerability.

### Pediatric Dosage (6 years and older) [US]
Weight-based titration:
- **Initial (Week 1):** 4.45 mg once daily.
- **Week 2:** Increase to 8.9 mg once daily.
- **Week 3:**
  - Patients <40 kg: Max dosage 17.8 mg once daily.
  - Patients ≥40 kg: Increase to 17.8 mg once daily.
- **Week 4 (Patients ≥40 kg only):** May increase to a maximum of 35.6 mg once daily.

### Dose Adjustments
- **CYP2D6 Poor Metabolizers:** 
  - Adults: Max 17.8 mg daily. 
  - Pediatrics: Max 8.9 mg (<40 kg) or 17.8 mg (≥40 kg).
- **Concomitant Strong CYP2D6 Inhibitors:** Reduce the dose of **WAKIX** by half for patients on a stable dose.
- **Concomitant Strong CYP3A4 Inducers:** May decrease exposure by 50%; consider doubling the dose of **WAKIX** over 7 days, then halving it if the inducer is discontinued.

### Missed Dose
- If a dose is missed, the patient should skip that dose and take the next dose the following morning at the usual time.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to pitolisant or any component of the formulation; anaphylaxis has been reported [US].
- Severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class C) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **QT Interval Prolongation:** **WAKIX** increases the QTc interval. Avoid use in patients with known QT prolongation, history of cardiac arrhythmias, or in combination with other drugs known to prolong the QT interval.
- **Electrolyte Imbalance:** Risk is increased in patients with hypokalemia or hypomagnesemia.
- **Hepatic/Renal Impairment:** Higher concentrations increase the risk of QTc prolongation; monitoring and dose adjustments are required (see Section 9).

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Adults
- **Common (≥5% and twice placebo):** Insomnia (6%), nausea (6%), and anxiety (5%) [US].
- **Other reactions (≥2%):** Headache (18%), upper respiratory tract infection (5%), musculoskeletal pain (5%), heart rate increased (3%), hallucinations (3%), irritability (3%), and abdominal pain (3%).

### Pediatrics
- **Common (≥5% and >placebo):** Headache (19%) and insomnia (7%) [US].

### Postmarketing Experience [US]
- **Immune System:** Anaphylaxis.
- **Psychiatric:** Abnormal dreams, bipolar disorder, depression, suicidal ideation/attempt.
- **Nervous System:** Epilepsy, dizziness.
- **General:** Fatigue, weight increase, pruritus.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Strong CYP2D6 Inhibitors (e.g., paroxetine):** Increase pitolisant exposure 2.2-fold. Reduce **WAKIX** dose by half.
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inducers (e.g., rifampin):** Decrease pitolisant exposure by 50%. Dose adjustment of **WAKIX** is required.
- **Sensitive CYP3A4 Substrates:** **WAKIX** is a weak inducer of CYP3A4 and may reduce the effectiveness of substrates, including hormonal contraceptives. Use alternative non-hormonal contraception during and for 21 days after stopping **WAKIX**.
- **H1 Receptor Antagonists:** Centrally acting antihistamines may reduce the effectiveness of **WAKIX**.
- **QTc-prolonging Drugs:** Avoid concomitant use.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **Category:** Not specified by letter category in the current US labeling.
  - **Data:** No drug-associated risk of major birth defects or miscarriage has been determined in humans. Animal data (rats/rabbits) showed embryofetal toxicity and malformations (cleft palate, abnormal limb flexure) at doses ≥13 times the Maximum Recommended Human Dose (MRHD).
  - **Registry:** A pregnancy exposure registry is available.
- **Breastfeeding:** Transfer into breastmilk is low (mean infant dose 0.009 mg/day; <1% of maternal weight-adjusted dose). Effects on the infant or milk production are unknown.
- **Contraception:** May reduce the efficacy of hormonal contraceptives; alternative methods are required during and for 21 days post-treatment.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for ages 6 years and older. Safety and efficacy not established in patients <6 years [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Limited data. Use caution and typically start at the lower end of the dosing range due to potential for decreased organ function.
- **Renal Impairment:**
  - eGFR 15–59 mL/min/1.73 m²: Adjust dose (Adult max 17.8 mg; Pediatric titration required).
  - ESRD (eGFR <15 mL/min/1.73 m²): Not recommended.
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
  - Mild (Child-Pugh A): No adjustment.
  - Moderate (Child-Pugh B): Adjust dose (Adult max 17.8 mg; Pediatric titration required).
  - Severe (Child-Pugh C): Contraindicated.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Antagonist/inverse agonist at the H3 receptor. While the exact mechanism in narcolepsy is unclear, it is thought to increase the synthesis and release of histamine in the brain.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** High affinity for H3 receptors (Ki = 1 nM). Minimal binding to H1, H2, or H4 receptors.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** 90% absorbed; median Tmax 3.5 hours. No clinically significant food effect.
  - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution ~700 L. Serum protein binding 91%–96%.
  - **Metabolism:** Primarily CYP2D6, minorly CYP3A4. Metabolites are inactive.
  - **Elimination:** Median half-life ~20 hours. 90% excreted in urine (mostly as metabolites); 2.3% in feces.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Adult Narcolepsy (Study 1 & 2):** Two 8-week trials showed significant reduction in Epworth Sleepiness Scale (ESS) scores compared to placebo (-3.1 and -2.2 difference, respectively).
- **Adult Cataplexy (Study 3 & 1):** Significant reduction in the weekly rate of cataplexy attacks (Rate Ratio 0.51 in Study 3).
- **Pediatric Narcolepsy (Study 4):** 8-week trial in patients 6–17 years showed significant improvement in the Pediatric Daytime Sleepiness Scale (PDSS) and reduction in cataplexy attacks (Rate Ratio 0.39).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F and 86°F).
- **Shelf Life:** Not specified in available sources.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only.
