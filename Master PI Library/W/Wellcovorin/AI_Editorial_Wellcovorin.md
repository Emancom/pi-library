# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis was developed using official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of **Levocarnitine** for use by healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a single, coherent reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s approved labelling for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (carrier molecule for fatty acid transport).
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection USP (Baxter) [US]. 
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion. 
    - **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. 
    - **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
    - **Max Dose:** The highest administered dose reported is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if levels are adequate.

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These can occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and initiate medical management [US].

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Seizure Risk:** Levocarnitine (oral or IV) has been associated with reports of seizures. This includes patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a known seizure history, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulation Note):** While this PI covers the injection, it is noted that chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted renally [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Plasma Concentrations:** It is recommended to obtain a baseline plasma carnitine concentration before starting parenteral therapy.
- **Ongoing Monitoring:** Weekly and monthly monitoring should include plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L), blood chemistries, and vital signs [US].

### Serious and Other Notable Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis and transient nausea/vomiting have been observed.
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (see Section 5).
- **Miscellaneous:** Body odor has been reported with levocarnitine use.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. **Clinical Management:** Monitor INR levels at treatment initiation and after dose adjustments [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly lettered, but categorised as "use only if clearly needed."
    - **Data:** Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after exogenous administration. In humans, the benefit to the mother must be weighed against potential risks to the infant from excess carnitine intake [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies revealed no evidence of impaired fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Used for inborn errors of metabolism. Secondary deficiency in premature infants and newborns is defined as plasma concentrations below age-related normal levels [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific dose adjustments are not provided in the source; however, general caution is advised regarding the higher frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function in this population.
- **Renal Impairment:** The injection is indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. Safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with renal insufficiency have not been evaluated [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Efficacy:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly after dialysis significantly increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Trough levels rose from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations at 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservative [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
