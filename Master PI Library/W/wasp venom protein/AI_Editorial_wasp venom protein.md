# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. At the time of compilation, only the United States (FDA) documentation was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** A naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Generic/Multi-source).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2–3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** In cases of severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours (q3h or q4h), and never less frequently than every 6 hours (q6h) [US].
- **Titration:** Subsequent daily doses are typically in the range of 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow 2–3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations <40–50 µmol/L. Adjustments (e.g., reduction to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough levels [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, often within minutes of administration. These occur primarily in patients with ESRD on dialysis. Discontinue immediately if a reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In those with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring:** Clinical monitoring should include periodic blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects listed below were reported in clinical trials for ESRD patients at a frequency ≥5%, regardless of causality [US].

| Body System | Adverse Event (Frequency ≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, palpitations |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, dyspepsia, anorexia |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia, hyperkalemia, peripheral edema, weight changes |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, depression, insomnia, paresthesia |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis |
| **Other** | Body odor (transient), gastritis, pruritus, rash, taste perversion |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis and other severe hypersensitivity reactions; seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. Frequent monitoring of INR is recommended upon initiation or dose adjustment of levocarnitine in patients on anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not explicitly assigned under current labeling, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm]. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations after exogenous administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility was observed in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy for metabolic disorders are established; follow weight-based dosing (50 mg/kg) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources; generally, dose selection should be cautious [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Carnitine deficiency is defined biochemically as plasma free carnitine <20 µmol/L at one week post-term [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include TMAO and γ-butyrobetaine.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Study:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg thrice weekly, trough levocarnitine levels increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. While carnitine concentrations were successfully increased, clinical outcomes regarding signs and symptoms of deficiency in this population have not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only. Poison schedule not applicable (Legend drug).
