# Editorial Product Information: WinRho SDF

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA). 
- **Note:** As only one jurisdiction's Product Information (PI) was provided for this synthesis, this editorial document reflects the regulatory requirements and clinical guidelines of the United States. Other jurisdictions may have different approved indications, dosing regimens, or safety warnings.
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is a synthesis of clinical and regulatory data and is not a substitute for the official PI used for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** $\text{Rh}_{\text{o}}$(D) Immune Globulin Intravenous (Human)
- **Chemical Class:** Plasma-derived gamma globulin (IgG) fraction containing antibodies to the $\text{Rh}_{\text{o}}$(D) antigen.
- **Brand Names:** WinRho SDF [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Ready-to-use liquid solution for injection in single-dose vials:
    - 600 IU (120 mcg)
    - 1,500 IU (300 mcg)
    - 2,500 IU (500 mcg)
    - 5,000 IU (1,000 mcg)
    - 15,000 IU (3,000 mcg)
- **Routes of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) or Intramuscular (IM). *Note: The IV route is mandatory for ITP indications.* [US]
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Kamada Ltd. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Immune Thrombocytopenic Purpura (ITP):** Indicated to raise platelet counts in $\text{Rh}_{\text{o}}$(D)-positive, non-splenectomized patients, including:
    - Children with acute or chronic ITP.
    - Adults with chronic ITP.
    - Children and adults with ITP secondary to HIV infection. [US]
- **Suppression of Rhesus (Rh) Isoimmunisation:**
    - Pregnancy and obstetric conditions in non-sensitised, $\text{Rh}_{\text{o}}$(D)-negative women with Rh-incompatible pregnancies (e.g., routine antepartum/postpartum prophylaxis, threatened abortion, amniocentesis).
    - Incompatible transfusions in $\text{Rh}_{\text{o}}$(D)-negative individuals exposed to $\text{Rh}_{\text{o}}$(D)-positive red blood cells (RBCs) or whole blood. [US]

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 Immune Thrombocytopenic Purpura (IV Route Only)
- **Initial Dosing:** 250 IU/kg (50 mcg/kg) body weight as a single injection over 3 to 5 minutes.
- **Dose Adjustments for Anemia:** If hemoglobin (Hgb) is 8 to <10 g/dL, reduce dose to 125–200 IU/kg (25–40 mcg/kg). Avoid use if Hgb is <8 g/dL. [US]
- **Subsequent Doses:** 125–300 IU/kg (25–60 mcg/kg) based on clinical response (platelet counts, Hgb, and reticulocyte levels). [US]

### 3.2 Suppression of Rh Isoimmunisation (IV or IM Route)
- **Routine Antepartum Prophylaxis:** 1,500 IU (300 mcg) at 28 weeks gestation.
- **Postpartum Prophylaxis:** 600 IU (120 mcg) within 72 hours of delivery of an $\text{Rh}_{\text{o}}$(D)-positive infant.
- **Obstetric Complications/Procedures:**
    - Threatened abortion/Amniocentesis (<34 weeks): 1,500 IU (300 mcg) immediately.
    - Abortion/Amniocentesis (>34 weeks): 600 IU (120 mcg) within 72 hours. [US]

### 3.3 Incompatible Transfusion
- **Intravenous:** 90 IU (18 mcg) per 1 mL of transfused RBCs, administered as 3,000 IU (600 mcg) every 8 hours until total dose is reached.
- **Intramuscular:** 120 IU (24 mcg) per 1 mL of transfused RBCs, administered as 6,000 IU (1,200 mcg) every 12 hours until total dose is reached. [US]

### 3.4 Administration Instructions
- Bring to room temperature before use. Inspect for particulate matter; do not use if cloudy.
- **Dilution:** If preferred for IV use, use **only** normal saline. Do **not** use Dextrose 5% (D5W).
- **Rate:** IV bolus over 3–5 minutes for ITP; 2 mL per 5–15 seconds for Rh prophylaxis. IM injections should be given in the deltoid or anterolateral thigh; avoid the gluteal region due to risk of sciatic nerve injury. [US]

## 4. Contraindications
- Known anaphylactic or severe systemic reaction to human immune globulin products. [US]
- IgA-deficient patients with antibodies against IgA or a history of hypersensitivity to **WinRho SDF** components. [US]
- Patients with a history of autoimmune hemolytic anemia or those at high risk for hemolysis. [US]
- Use in infants for the suppression of $\text{Rh}_{\text{o}}$(D) isoimmunisation. [US]

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **BOXED WARNING (ITP ONLY): INTRAVASCULAR HEMOLYSIS (IVH)**
    - IVH leading to death has been reported in ITP patients. This can result in severe anemia, multi-system organ failure (including ARDS), acute renal failure, and disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC).
    - **Monitoring:** Patients must be monitored in a healthcare setting for at least 8 hours post-infusion. Perform dipstick urinalysis at baseline, 2 hours, 4 hours, and prior to discharge.
    - **Post-treatment:** Advise patients to monitor for back pain, chills, fever, or discolored urine for at least 72 hours. [US]
- **Hypersensitivity:** Severe reactions, including anaphylaxis, may occur. Discontinue infusion immediately if symptoms (e.g., urticaria, chest tightness, wheezing, hypotension) arise. [US]
- **Renal Insufficiency:** Risk of acute renal failure and osmotic nephropathy, particularly in patients with pre-existing renal disease, diabetes, or those over 65 years. Administer at the minimum rate practicable. [US]
- **Thrombotic Events:** May occur regardless of the route of administration. Monitor patients with known risk factors (e.g., atherosclerosis, hyperviscosity). [US]
- **Transmissible Infectious Agents:** As a plasma-derived product, there is a theoretical risk of transmitting viruses or the Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease (CJD) agent. [US]
- **Blood Glucose Interference:** Contains 10% maltose. This can cause falsely high readings in certain glucose monitoring systems (e.g., GDH-PQQ methods), leading to life-threatening untreated hypoglycemia if insulin is inappropriately administered. Use only glucose-specific testing systems. [US]

## 6. Adverse Effects
### 6.1 Clinical Trial Experience (ITP)
- **Very Common (>10%):** Headache (11%), Fever (11%), Asthenia (11%). [US]
- **Common (1–10%):** Chills (8%), Dizziness (4%), Infection (3%). [US]
- **Other reactions (≤2%):** Pallor, diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, arthralgia, myalgia, malaise, abdominal/back pain, hypotension/hypertension, increased LDH, somnolence, and rash. [US]

### 6.2 Suppression of Rh Isoimmunisation
- Adverse reactions are rare (<0.1%) in this population. [US]

### 6.3 Post-marketing/Serious Adverse Effects
- **Blood:** IVH, DIC, hemoglobinemia.
- **Cardiac/Respiratory:** Cardiac arrest, myocardial infarction, TRALI, ARDS, dyspnea.
- **Renal:** Acute renal failure, anuria, chromaturia, hemoglobinuria. [US]

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Live Virus Vaccines:** Immunoglobulin administration may impair the efficacy of live attenuated vaccines (e.g., measles, mumps, rubella, varicella). Delay vaccination for at least 3 months following **WinRho SDF** administration. [US]
- **Serological Testing:** Passive transfer of antibodies may result in transiently positive results in serological tests (e.g., Coombs' test, anti-Duffy, anti-Kidd). [US]

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not specified in source]. Clinical trials for Rh isoimmunisation suppression showed no harm to the fetus or newborn. For ITP treatment, no human or animal data are available to assess drug-associated risk. [US]
- **Breastfeeding:** Presence in human milk is unknown. Consider the developmental/health benefits of breastfeeding alongside the mother’s clinical need and potential adverse effects on the infant. [US]
- **Fertility:** Data not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Evaluated for ITP in children (<16 years). Dosing is identical to adult dosing. [US]
- **Elderly Use (>65 years):** Insufficient data from clinical trials. Post-marketing data indicate that elderly patients with comorbidities are at a higher risk of fatal outcomes from IVH and acute renal failure. Use caution and consider starting at the lower end of the dosing range. [US]
- **Renal Impairment:** Patients at risk for renal failure should be monitored for BUN and serum creatinine; dose at the minimum infusion rate practicable. [US]
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **ITP:** Postulated that anti-D binds to $\text{Rh}_{\text{o}}$(D)-positive RBCs, creating antibody-coated complexes that are preferentially removed by the spleen. This results in Fc receptor blockade, which spares antibody-coated platelets from destruction.
    - **Rh Isoimmunisation:** Suppresses the immune response of $\text{Rh}_{\text{o}}$(D)-negative individuals to $\text{Rh}_{\text{o}}$(D)-positive RBCs. [US]
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **IV:** Peak levels reached within 2 hours. Half-life is approximately 24 days.
    - **IM:** Peak levels reached in 5–10 days. Half-life is approximately 30 days. [US]

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ITP:** In a study of 24 children with chronic ITP, 79% responded with a platelet increase to $\geq 50,000/\text{mm}^3$. In adults with chronic ITP (n=24), the response rate was 88%.
- **Rh Isoimmunisation:** In a trial of 1,186 $\text{Rh}_{\text{o}}$(D)-negative pregnant women, the combination of antenatal and postnatal administration reduced the isoimmunisation rate to approximately 0.1%. [US]

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F). [US]
- **Handling:** Do not freeze. Protect from light. Do not use beyond expiration date. [US]
- **Shelf Life:** Not specified in available sources.
