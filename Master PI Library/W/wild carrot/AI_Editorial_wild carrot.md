# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesised from the following jurisdiction-specific Product Information (PI): United States (FDA). 
- This document serves as a harmonised clinical overview intended for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (carrier molecule).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inborn metabolic errors [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dose:** Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg [US].
- **Administration:** Delivered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Severe Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Frequency should be every 3 or 4 hours (q3h or q4h), and never less than every 6 hours (q6h) [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest administered dose reported is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration Timing:** Administered as a slow bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration [US].
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and initiate medical management [US].
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations before starting parenteral therapy. Periodic monitoring (weekly/monthly) should include blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels (target range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Oral Formulation Caution:** Safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. High-dose oral administration in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Neurologic:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis and respiratory distress (laryngeal edema/bronchospasm) [US].

### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported with a frequency ≥5% in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials, regardless of causality, include:

| Body System | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), headache (22%), flu syndrome (25%), asthenia (9%), abdominal pain (9%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), chest pain (12%) |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), anorexia (5%) |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%), rhinitis (9%) |
| **Neurological** | Dizziness (13%), paresthesia (5%), insomnia (4%) |
| **Other** | Hypercalcemia (9%), anemia (6%), pruritus (5%) |

*Note: Transient nausea and vomiting have been observed. Less frequent reactions include body odor and gastritis [US].*

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating treatment or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified by letter, but indicated for use "only if clearly needed"]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits using up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. There are no adequate/controlled studies in pregnant women [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after exogenous administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake to the child must be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg) [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** See "Warnings and Precautions" regarding the accumulation of metabolites with oral dosing. For IV dosing in ESRD, see "Dosage and Administration" [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources; however, ESRD trials included adult populations where dosage adjustments were guided by plasma concentrations [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for beta-oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, levocarnitine administration corrects the low plasma carnitine levels and high acylcarnitine/carnitine ratios caused by reduced intake, reduced renal synthesis, and dialytic losses [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Excretion:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg IV three times weekly), mean pre-dialysis plasma concentrations increased from 20 µmol/L at baseline to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks. 
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** In a study (N≈30 per group) comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg, trough concentrations remained stable or increased over 24 weeks, with the 40 mg/kg group reaching 456 ± 162 µmol/L [US].
- **Clinical Outcomes Note:** While IV levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations, the effect on specific clinical outcomes or signs/symptoms of deficiency in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat and protect from freezing.
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) [US].

- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available US sources.
