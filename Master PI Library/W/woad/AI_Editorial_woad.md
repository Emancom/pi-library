# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: US (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a single clinical reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s approved labelling for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (generic name); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Quaternary ammonium compound; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
    - **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; frequency should never be less than every 6 hours [US].
    - **Maximum Dose:** Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Dose Adjustments:** Based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels (target: 40–50 µmol/L). Adjustments (e.g., downward to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard unused portions as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of intravenous administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue immediately and consider the risks/benefits before any re-challenge [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine may increase the frequency or severity of seizures in patients with pre-existing seizure disorders. Seizures have also been reported in patients without a prior history of epilepsy [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:**
    - Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels.
    - Monitor weekly and monthly; include blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].
    - Monitor International Normalised Ratio (INR) in patients co-administered with warfarin [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency from Clinical Trials (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events occurring at ≥5% in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials:

| Body System | Adverse Event | Frequency (Levocarnitine 10–40 mg/kg) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Injection site reaction, Pain, Flu syndrome, Chest pain, Headache, Infection, Abdominal pain, Fever | Common (>10%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, Hypotension, Tachycardia | Common (>10%) |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, Vomiting, Nausea | Common (>10%) |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, Cough increase, Dyspnea, Rhinitis | Common (>10%) |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness | Common (>10%) |
| **Musculoskeletal** | Leg cramps | Common (1–10%) |

### Serious and Other Adverse Effects
- **Serious:** Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, and seizures.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis (less frequent).
- **Other:** Transient nausea/vomiting and a distinct "fishy" body odor have been observed [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased INR have been observed. Recommendation: Monitor INR levels closely upon initiation or dose adjustment of levocarnitine in patients on anticoagulant therapy [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly lettered, but animal studies in rats and rabbits (up to 3.8 times the human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. In dairy cows, carnitine concentrations in milk increase following administration. Weigh the benefits of maternal treatment against the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no evidence of impaired fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg); see Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency.
    - Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites: trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted renally [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly eliminated [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, IV administration increases plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Normal plasma concentrations are defined as >20 µmol/L; targets during therapy are typically 35–60 µmol/L [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours.
    - **Half-life:** Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD PK Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg post-dialysis), mean pre-dialysis (trough) levels increased from a baseline of 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg showed dose-proportional increases in trough levocarnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks.
- **Note:** While IV levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD, the impact on specific clinical outcomes or signs/symptoms of carnitine deficiency in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability (Admixture):** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
