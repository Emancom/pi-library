# Editorial Product Information: Wegovy (semaglutide)

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (TGA), US (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of official Product Information (PI) from multiple regulatory authorities. it is intended for use by healthcare professionals as a comprehensive clinical overview.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial synthesis is not a substitute for the official PI or prescribing information required for clinical practice in a specific jurisdiction. Always consult the current local PI before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Semaglutide (rys), a human glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonist.
- **Brand Names:** **Wegovy** (Injection and Tablets [US]); **Wegovy FlexTouch** (Injection).
- **ATC Code:** A10BJ06.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Solution for Injection:** 0.25 mg, 0.5 mg, 1 mg, 1.7 mg, 2.4 mg, and 7.2 mg [US: Wegovy HD] per dose. Available in single-dose pens, single-dose syringes, and multi-dose FlexTouch pens (delivering four 2.4 mg doses) [Australian, US].
    - **Oral Tablets:** 1.5 mg, 4 mg, 9 mg, and 25 mg [US-approved; not specified in Australian source].
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous (Injection); Oral (Tablets [US]).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Novo Nordisk.

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chronic Weight Management:** As an adjunct to a reduced-energy/calorie diet and increased physical activity for long-term weight reduction/maintenance in:
    - Adults with an initial BMI $\ge$ 30 kg/m² (obesity).
    - Adults with an initial BMI $\ge$ 27 kg/m² (overweight) with at least one weight-related comorbidity (e.g., hypertension, dyslipidaemia, type 2 diabetes).
    - Pediatric patients aged 12 years and older with obesity (BMI $\ge$ 95th percentile for age/sex) and weight $> 60$ kg [Australian].
- **Reduction in Risk of Major Adverse Cardiovascular Events (MACE):** To reduce the risk of CV death, non-fatal myocardial infarction, or non-fatal stroke in adults with established cardiovascular disease and obesity or overweight (BMI $\ge$ 27 kg/m²) [Australian, US].
- **Metabolic Dysfunction-Associated Steatohepatitis (MASH):** Treatment of non-cirrhotic MASH in adults with moderate to advanced liver fibrosis (stages F2 to F3).
    - *Regulatory Status:* This indication received **Provisional Approval** [Australian] / **Accelerated Approval** [US] based on the resolution of steatohepatitis and improvement in fibrosis. Continued approval depends on verification of clinical benefit in confirmatory trials.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 Subcutaneous Injection (Adults and Pediatrics 12+)
- **Administration:** Once weekly, any time of day, with or without meals. Inject into the abdomen, thigh, or upper arm.
- **Titration Schedule:** Follow a 16-week escalation to minimize gastrointestinal (GI) symptoms:
    - Weeks 1–4: 0.25 mg weekly.
    - Weeks 5–8: 0.5 mg weekly.
    - Weeks 9–12: 1 mg weekly.
    - Weeks 13–16: 1.7 mg weekly.
    - **Maintenance:** 2.4 mg weekly.
- **Dose Adjustments:**
    - If 2.4 mg is not tolerated, the dose may be reduced to 1.7 mg weekly [Australian, US].
    - For adults with BMI $\ge$ 30 kg/m² requiring additional weight loss, the dose may be increased to 7.2 mg weekly (administered as three 2.4 mg injections) after at least 4 weeks on the 2.4 mg dose [Australian, US].
- **Missed Dose:** If missed, administer as soon as possible within 5 days. If $> 5$ days have passed, skip the dose and resume on the next regularly scheduled day. If $\ge 2$ consecutive doses are missed, consider re-initiating titration at a lower dose.

### 3.2 Oral Tablets [US]
- **Administration:** Once daily on an empty stomach upon awakening with up to 4 ounces (approx. 120 mL) of plain water. Swallow whole. Wait at least 30 minutes before eating, drinking, or taking other oral medications.
- **Titration Schedule:**
    - Days 1–30: 1.5 mg daily.
    - Days 31–60: 4 mg daily.
    - Days 61–90: 9 mg daily.
    - **Maintenance:** 25 mg daily.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Personal or family history of Medullary Thyroid Carcinoma (MTC) or Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2) [US-specific contraindication; listed as a warning in Australian source].**
- Known hypersensitivity to semaglutide or any excipients.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning: Risk of Thyroid C-cell Tumours [US]:** Semaglutide causes dose-dependent and treatment-duration-dependent thyroid C-cell tumours in rodents. It is unknown whether it causes MTC in humans. Patients should be counselled on the risk and symptoms of thyroid tumours (e.g., neck mass, dysphagia).
- **Acute Pancreatitis:** Observed in clinical trials. Discontinue immediately if pancreatitis is suspected; do not restart if confirmed.
- **Acute Gallbladder Disease:** Increased risk of cholelithiasis and cholecystitis. Substantial weight loss may also contribute.
- **Hypoglycaemia:** Risk increases when used with insulin or insulin secretagogues (e.g., sulfonylureas). Consider dose reduction of concomitant antidiabetics.
- **Acute Kidney Injury (AKI):** Reports of AKI and worsening of chronic renal failure, often associated with GI-induced dehydration. Monitor renal function in patients with severe GI reactions.
- **Diabetic Retinopathy Complications:** Rapid improvement in glucose control has been associated with temporary worsening of diabetic retinopathy. Monitor patients with a history of the condition.
- **Non-Arteritic Anterior Ischaemic Optic Neuropathy (NAION) [Australian]:** Epidemiological studies indicate an increased risk of NAION. Urgently refer patients with sudden vision loss to an ophthalmologist and discontinue treatment if confirmed.
- **Suicidal Behaviour and Ideation:** Monitor for emergence or worsening of depression or suicidal thoughts. Discontinue if symptoms develop [Australian]. Note: Suicidal behaviour warning was removed from the US Highlights in 2026.
- **Gastrointestinal Disease:** Not recommended in patients with severe gastroparesis.
- **Aspiration Risk:** Increased risk of residual gastric content during general anaesthesia or deep sedation. Consider this risk prior to procedures.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are primarily gastrointestinal and most frequent during the titration phase.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Nausea, diarrhoea, vomiting, constipation, abdominal pain, headache, fatigue. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Dizziness, dyspepsia, abdominal distension, eructation, flatulence, gastro-oesophageal reflux disease, gastritis, hair loss (alopecia), injection site reactions. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Hypotension, orthostatic hypotension, increased heart rate, syncope, appendicitis. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Anaphylactic reaction, acute pancreatitis. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, AKI, anaphylaxis, NAION [Australian], and potential MTC.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Delayed Gastric Emptying:** Semaglutide slows gastric emptying, which may impact the absorption of concomitantly administered oral medications. 
- **Levothyroxine [US]:** Oral semaglutide increased levothyroxine exposure by 33%. Monitor thyroid parameters.
- **Narrow Therapeutic Index Drugs:** Consider increased monitoring for oral medications requiring rapid absorption or having a narrow therapeutic index.
- **Warfarin:** Frequent monitoring of INR is recommended upon initiation in patients on warfarin or coumarin derivatives [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:**
    - **Australian Category:** **Category D** (Suspected to cause fetal harm/malformations).
    - **General Advice:** Discontinue **Wegovy** when pregnancy is recognized. Due to its long half-life, it should be discontinued at least 2 months before a planned pregnancy.
- **Breastfeeding:** Semaglutide is excreted in the milk of lactating rats. A risk to the breastfed child cannot be excluded. **Wegovy** tablets are not recommended during breastfeeding [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed subclinical effects on oestrous length and ovulation in females at doses associated with maternal weight loss. No effects on male fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatrics:** Approved for weight management in children $\ge$ 12 years with obesity [Australian, US]. Safety and efficacy not established for children $< 12$ years or for the MASH indication.
- **Elderly ($\ge$ 65 years):** No general dose adjustment required. Therapeutic experience in patients $\ge$ 85 years is limited.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for mild, moderate, or severe impairment. Experience is limited in severe impairment (CrCL $< 30$ mL/min); not recommended for end-stage renal disease.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment required. Caution is advised in severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C).

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A GLP-1 analogue that binds to and activates the GLP-1 receptor. It regulates appetite by increasing feelings of fullness and satiety while lowering hunger and cravings. It also stimulates insulin secretion and lowers glucagon in a glucose-dependent manner.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is 89% (Injection) and 1–2% (Tablets [US]). Steady state is reached after 4–5 weeks of weekly administration.
    - **Distribution:** Extensively bound to plasma albumin ($> 99\%$).
    - **Metabolism:** Proteolytic cleavage of the peptide backbone and beta-oxidation of the fatty acid side chain.
    - **Elimination:** Half-life is approximately 1 week. Semaglutide may remain in the circulation for about 7 weeks after the last dose.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **STEP Programme (Weight Management):** In 68-week trials, semaglutide 2.4 mg weekly resulted in statistically significant weight loss vs placebo (e.g., -14.9% vs -2.4% in STEP 1). 
- **SELECT (Cardiovascular Outcomes):** A trial of 17,604 adults with established CVD and BMI $\ge$ 27 (without diabetes) showed a 20% reduction in MACE (Hazard Ratio 0.80) over a median 40 months.
- **ESSENCE (MASH):** Interim 72-week data showed superior resolution of steatohepatitis (62.9% vs 34.3%) and improvement in fibrosis (36.8% vs 22.4%) compared to placebo.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Injection:** Store in a refrigerator ($2^\circ\text{C}$ to $8^\circ\text{C}$). Do not freeze. 
    - *Unrefrigerated Storage:* Single-dose pens/syringes may be kept at room temperature (up to $30^\circ\text{C}$) for up to 28 days. FlexTouch pens may be kept unrefrigerated for up to 42 days [Australian] or 56 days [US].
- **Tablets [US]:** Store at $20^\circ\text{C}$ to $25^\circ\text{C}$ in the original bottle to protect from moisture.
