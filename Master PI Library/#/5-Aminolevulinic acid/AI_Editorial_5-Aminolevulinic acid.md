# Editorial Product Information: 5-Aminolevulinic acid

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** 5-Aminolevulinic acid (as hydrochloride).
- **Chemical Class:** Sensitiser used in photodynamic/radiation therapy.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Alacare** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01XD04.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Dermal patch containing 8 mg of 5-aminolevulinic acid (as hydrochloride) per 4 $cm^2$ (equivalent to 2 mg per $cm^2$).
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Topical (dermal).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Link Medical Products PTY LTD [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- Treatment of mild to moderate actinic keratoses (AK) lesions located on the face and the scalp (specifically hairless areas) [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults and Elderly):** A single photodynamic therapy (PDT) session involves the application of the dermal patches. A maximum of eight (8) patches may be used on eight different lesions during a single session.
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Patches must cover the lesion completely; adhesive strips may be used if the patch does not adhere well.
    - **Patch Duration:** Patches are to remain in place for four (4) hours.
    - **Illumination:** After removing the patch, the lesion(s) must be exposed to a red light source (narrow-band LED, 630 ± 3 nm) with a total light dose of 37 $J/cm^2$.
    - **Protection:** Only lamps with filters/mirrors to minimise heat and UV radiation should be used. Both patients and operators must wear protective goggles corresponding to the lamp’s spectrum. 
    - Untreated skin surrounding the lesion does not require protection.
- **Disposal:** Used patches should be folded in half (adhesive side inwards) and discarded safely.
- **Assessment:** Clinical response of the lesions should be evaluated three months post-treatment.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to 5-aminolevulinic acid or any components of the patch material.
- History of non-responsiveness to previous photodynamic therapy using 5-aminolevulinic acid.
- Porphyria.
- Known photodermatoses (various types including metabolic disorders like aminoaciduria, idiopathic/immunological disorders like polymorphic light reaction, or genetic disorders such as xeroderma pigmentosum).
- Diseases aggravated by sunlight, such as lupus erythematosus or pemphigus erythematosus.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources.
- **General Warnings:**
    - **Clinical Limitations:** Highly hyperkeratotic (thick, red, scaly) AK lesions should not be treated with this product.
    - **Skin Type:** Use is not recommended in patients with Fitzpatrick skin types IV to VI (moderate brown to black skin), as clinical data for this population is absent and AK is rare in these groups.
    - **Professional Use:** Administration should only be performed by healthcare professionals trained in photodynamic therapy.
- **Monitoring and Site Precautions:**
    - **Sun Exposure:** Patients must avoid sunlight/UV exposure to the treated site and surrounding skin for approximately 48 hours following treatment.
    - **Pre-existing Conditions:** Treatment efficacy and assessment may be hindered by local skin diseases (psoriasis, eczema, infection, or malignancy) and tattoos.
    - **Ocular Safety:** Avoid direct eye contact with the patch.
    - **UV Therapy:** Discontinue any UV-therapy prior to starting treatment.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **General Profile:** Nearly all patients (99%) experience local adverse reactions at the treatment site, primarily due to phototoxic effects. 
- **Treatment Phases:** 
    - *During application:* 33% of patients report pruritus, burning, or erythema. 
    - *During illumination:* Erythema, burning, and pain are most common. 1% of patients require early termination of light therapy due to pain.
    - *Post-therapy:* Pruritus, erythema, scabbing, and exfoliation are frequent, usually mild to moderate, and persist for 1 to 2 weeks.

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Erythema, exfoliation, irritation, pain, pruritus, scab. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Bleeding, desquamation, discharge, discomfort, erosion, hyper/hypopigmentation, oedema, swelling, vesicles, pustules, headache. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Burn, discolouration, excoriation, inflammation, ulcer, infection, pyoderma, emotional distress, epistaxis, increased alanine aminotransferase. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Phototoxic Potentiators:** Concomitant use of agents with known phototoxic or photoallergic potential (e.g., St. John’s Wort, griseofulvin, thiazide diuretics, sulfonylureas, phenothiazines, sulphonamides, quinolones, and tetracyclines) may enhance the PDT reaction.
- **Hypericin:** St John’s Wort products should be discontinued two weeks prior to treatment.
- **Potential Potentiators (Nonclinical):** Phototoxicity may be increased by etretinate, methotrexate, vitamin D analogues, or iron chelators.
- **ROS Scavengers:** Efficacy may be reduced by dietary supplements that act as reactive oxygen species scavengers (e.g., tryptophan, glutathione, N-acetylcysteine, melatonin, and methionine).
- **Topical Products:** Concomitant use with other topical medications should be avoided.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B2.** 
    - Animal studies (rabbits) showed no embryofetal developmental toxicity at doses 280 times the human systemic exposure. 
    - However, there are no adequate data in pregnant women. Use is not recommended unless clearly necessary.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if 5-aminolevulinic acid is excreted in human milk. As a precaution, breastfeeding should be suspended for 48 hours following patch application.
- **Fertility:** No specific human data. Animal studies showed no effect at therapeutic doses, though a transient reduction in male fertility was seen at toxic systemic doses (1000 mg/kg).

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients aged 0 to 18 years have not been established. No data are available.
- **Elderly Use:** No special dose adjustments or instructions are required for patients over 65 years.
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 5-aminolevulinic acid is a precursor of Protoporphyrin IX (PPIX). After topical application, PPIX accumulates intracellularly within AK lesions. Upon activation by red light in the presence of oxygen, singlet oxygen is produced, causing mitochondrial and cellular damage to the target cells.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** PPIX-specific fluorescence is significantly higher in AK lesions than in normal skin and increases with exposure duration up to 4 hours.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Following the application of 8 patches for 4 hours, the baseline corrected $C_{max}$ of systemic 5-ALA was 16.4 µg/L, with $T_{max}$ at 4 hours.
    - **Distribution:** PPIX was not detected in plasma samples.
    - **Elimination:** Urinary excretion of 5-ALA is low, with a median of 1.39% of the dose excreted within the first 12 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Dose-Finding:** A study of 160 patients confirmed the optimal patch application duration is 4 hours.
- **Efficacy vs. Placebo:** In a trial of 107 patients, a single PDT session with Alacare was statistically superior to placebo-PDT at 12 weeks for clearing mild-to-moderate AK lesions on the head/face.
- **Efficacy vs. Cryosurgery:** An open randomised trial (n=349) demonstrated that Alacare-PDT was superior to cryosurgery. At 12 weeks, the lesion clearance rate was 87% for Alacare-PDT compared to 77% for cryosurgery (p=0.007). The recurrence rate at 12 months was lower for Alacare-PDT (12%) compared to cryosurgery (18%).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Shelf Life/Handling:** After opening the protective sachet, patches must be used within 3 months. 

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Prescription Only Medicine (S4) [Australian].
